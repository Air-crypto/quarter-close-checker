\section{Changes made on development data, version by version}\label{sup:changes}
The full record behind the versions table of Appendix~\ref{app:changes} of the paper.

\textbf{\vq{2}} (synthetic dev bundles 00--01). A lone dash followed by words is not a zero. A prose reader takes only the number printed right after the label phrase. Digit-bearing tokens in a label or entity (invoice numbers, dates) must be printed on the matched line. A heading must print no values, labels that name the entity win, and a column read must sit under its header cell. New schema options: a \code{dot\_thousands} number format and a per-field scale. Misread repair works at the extracted value's decimal precision, and tier~2 suggestions are never applied internally. On dev, text-mode breaks fell from 82 to 0 and tier~2 items from 207 to 37.

\textbf{\vq{3}} (CORD train and validation; 10 SEC dev filings). A \code{grouped} number format; unit statements in parentheses; a page with no unit statement inherits the nearest earlier page's; date and stacked headers; a more specific label claims its line; a label that finds its line but cannot read it vetoes the field; label-less entity rows are not read; swaps and flips are confirmed on the fields' own lines; and the optional \code{label\_read: backed}. A CORD adapter assigns each shared receipt-level label to the field that carries it most often on TRAIN. On dev, \vq{3} broke 0 numbers in every group and mode, where \vq{2} broke 1,817 on CORD train gold extractions and 11 on each clean SEC dev extraction.

\textbf{\vq{4}} (SEC development filings: their clean and poor-scan extractions, OCR of their poor scans, injected errors; CORD train and validation; regression on synthetic dev bundles and the PE validation). An OCR mode with the two-reader rule of Section~\ref{sec:textmode}, a per-document scale vote, and OCR cleanup (rows fitted along the page slope, specks dropped, words snapped to the label vocabulary, header years snapped, missing parentheses restored, a punctuation mark before three final digits read as a thousands comma). Product terms in identities, and the CORD adapter's line identity rewritten from $\text{price}-c\cdot\text{unit price}$ with the true count $c$ to $\text{price}-\text{count}\times\text{unit price}$. A row read for label-less entity rows. No sign flip away from a declared sign. Identity-localised flags. A threshold to flag high-confidence OCR disagreements was tried at 0.9 and 0.97, added flags without catching an error, and is off. On the development filings \vq{4} broke no number in any mode, where \vq{3} fed OCR as a text layer broke 86 to 135 per run (Table~\ref{tab:q4dev}); in text mode its fixes, silent and broken counts equal \vq{3}'s and only the flags shrink. On CORD development data it made no wrong or broken change; a broken count found during tuning (a count read from the previous item's continuation row) was fixed by preferring the continuation row below.

\textbf{\vq{5}} (development tables of the notes benchmark, 10 filers, 76 tables, 6,589 numbers, with the Opus, Haiku and GPT extractions and injected errors; SEC development poor scans, their OCR and injected errors; CORD train and validation; synthetic dev bundles 00--01; PE validation). It changes only the page read. A read changes a value only under rule (a), identity-backed, or rule (b), unambiguous and corroborated (Section~\ref{sec:labelread}); every other read is a tier~2 item with the reason it was not taken. The guard runs jointly over all documents, and when an identity that tied breaks, the one other input whose page cell makes it tie again completes the correction. A read never changes a value by a power of 1000. Unit statements in billions and unit statements broken over two lines are read, and a line running across the row-label edge ends a stacked header. Tried and rejected on development data: rule (b) alone with \vq{4}'s per-document guard (notes breaks 0, but PE validation fixes fell from 4,718 to 1,411), and using a voted page scale to read values in text mode (12 wrong changes on the PE validation).

\textbf{\vq{6}} (the \vq{5} development data, plus the real-scan development split and real re-reads of development crops by Haiku and Opus readers, two rounds). The weaknesses addressed were low identity coverage, OCR and a scan's text layer posing as a trustworthy page, errors that agree or cancel, and the hand-built files every domain needed. Changes: zero-config identity discovery and schema inference (Section~\ref{sec:discover}); the re-read tier (Section~\ref{sec:rereadmethod}); a scanned PDF's text layer treated as an OCR (on the real-scan development split \vq{5} broke 4 correct numbers per model in text mode, \vq{6} none); identity coverage reported per run. Tried and not kept: a uniqueness filter on discovery's page reads (cut SEC coverage from 82\% to 78\%); the backed label read for every zero-config run (SEC zero-config fixes fell from 57 to 20); equality ties without the year check (paired columns of different years); zero-config on CORD with the replace-style label read and free signs (23 of 8,405 correct numbers broken on CORD train); exempting every page-confirmed label from the per-document check (12 broken on the notes development tables); and the first re-read gate (Section~\ref{sec:reread}). With hand files, \vq{6} gives \vq{5}'s results on every development set except scanned text layers. Source: \code{qcore6/CHANGES.md}, \code{out/qcore6\_work/dev\_summary6.md}.

\textbf{\vq{7}} (every \vq{6} held-out set, now spent: SEC sets 1--3, CORD test, the notes tables, the real scans, synthetic 02--05; and \vq{6}'s development grid). The weaknesses addressed, measured on those sets: zero-config broke correct numbers (age bands read as values; a discovered identity that held by accident backing a change) and left more numbers silent than hand identities (fewer identities discovered; without page text, on scanned forms and receipts, almost no flags). The changes are the four of Section~\ref{sec:discover}, all gated on something being discovered; nine tests were added. Costs on development data: more flags on scanned forms, unchanged no-text results on SEC filings, and fewer fixes, because changes resting only on unbacked discovered identities become suggestions. Source: \code{qcore7/CHANGES.md}, \code{out/qcore7\_work/dev\_summary7.md} (Section~\ref{app:q7dev}).

\textbf{\vq{8}} (everything above, plus set~H, \vq{7}'s fresh synthetic set, spent by \vq{7}'s run). The weaknesses addressed, measured on those sets: the two notes-table breaks of zero-config \vq{7}, and zero-config leaving 3 to 9 times as many numbers silent as hand identities on set~H, mostly because discovery missed long totals in error-heavy documents, cross-document ties and products with rates. The changes are the five of Section~\ref{sec:discover}, all in zero-config code; six tests were added and one \vq{7} test changed on purpose (a digit misread under a weak label now goes to tier~2). Three guards in the inferred schema's exact-label rule were added after development breaks: a long label whose words a shorter line also prints (two SEC development numbers, before the first freeze), a footnote marker after the label (one notes-table number per model, first freeze), and fewer than three significant digits (two SEC set~3 numbers, second freeze). Tried and not kept: naming every other unconfirmed input of a discovered identity when a misread suggestion rests on discovered identities alone (set~H high, no text, 73 to 68 silent, but more flags on clean extractions). Costs on development data: CORD validation zero-config silent 25 to 31, fewer fixes on SEC sets 2 and 3, more flags on clean data, and slower discovery (322 to 350\,s for the largest development run against 133\,s). Source: \code{qcore8/CHANGES.md}, \code{out/qcore8\_work/dev\_summary8.md} (Section~\ref{app:q8dev}).

\textbf{\vq{9}} (everything above, plus set~I, \vq{8}'s fresh set, spent by \vq{8}'s run). The weaknesses addressed, measured there: \vq{8}'s zero-config breaks (9 bank-statement checks printed twice with opposite signs; 7 cells of an inventory row read from a neighbour whose numbers include the record's identifier; one week-total completion on the wrong day), one hand-mode and one discovered-identity break from loose scaled page matches (a budget report storing values at the page's own unit), and the gap to hand identities without page text on the SEC development sets. The changes are the five of Section~\ref{sec:discover}; eight tests were added. Measured one at a time on SEC development sets in zero-config (silent / false flags over 12 runs per mode): slip admission, without page text $-13$ / $+11$ (with it $-7$ / $+109$, so off with page text); tight tolerance $-28$ / $+116$ (with page text $-3$ / $+56$, off); section-total sums $-7$ / $+10$; equal ties $-17$ / $+15$; keeping a rule-(b) read's page basis raised text-mode fixes from 1,161 to 1,276. Tried and not kept: the two discovery checks with page text; the tight tolerance at 2.5 units ($-3$ silent); a first version of change~3 that required every scaled match to be at least one page unit, which stopped confirming SEC sub-unit lines and added 54 hand-mode flags on development sets. Not done: a generic rule for the two hand-mode breaks \vq{8} already had on development data (the Snap-on swap on SEC set~3 and the Salesforce cell on the notes tables). Source: \code{qcore9/CHANGES.md}, \code{out/qcore9\_work/FROZEN.md}.
